What Orbit is

Orbit is a single-page web app: one sign-in portal at / for everyone in the company, plus an invite-only registration page at /signup where new hires complete the account their admin created. What you see after signing in is decided by your role — the same shell shows an employee their timer, leaves and calendar; a manager additionally gets approvals and announcements; an admin gets the full people, policy and settings toolkit. Every permission is enforced on the server, not just hidden in the UI. (The public marketing site is a separate project — this app is purely the workspace.)

⏱

Attendance timer

A Zoho-style check-in timer records your day from server-side events: check in, pause for breaks, resume, check out. It survives refreshes and re-logins because elapsed time is always computed on the server.

🌿

Leave management

Sick, casual and earned leave with live balances. Full days, half days, date ranges or hand-picked custom dates (up to 31 per request) — requests go to your immediate manager and balances are deducted only on approval.

🏠

Work from home

A location change, not time off — the approval flow is identical to leave but no balance is touched. A reason is required so the approving manager has context.

✅

Approvals

Managers get a live queue of pending leave and WFH from their direct reports only, with identity, dates and balance context on every card — approve or reject with an optional comment.

📣

Announcements

Admins and managers compose announcements — company-wide, per role, to a reporting subtree, or to custom teams — and can track read state on everything they've sent.

🔔

Notifications

A dedicated notifications page plus a quick-glance bell drawer, with unread badges in the sidebar. Pending work and received announcements land in one feed.

👥

People directory

Search every employee with designation, department, manager, employee ID and photo. Admins onboard people, set roles and reassign managers (cycle-safe) right from the roster.

🏢

Organization tree

Built purely from each person's immediate manager and updated the moment someone is added or reassigned. Nodes show live presence — online, idle or offline — derived from today's timer activity.

📅

Shared calendar

Approved leave and WFH plotted month by month so anyone can see team availability at a glance — auto-leave verdicts from the attendance rule show up here too.

⚙️

Leave policies

Admins manage leave types, annual quotas and employment types on a dedicated page; balances update everywhere the moment a policy or approval changes.

📊

Attendance analytics

Admin views of all attendance and all leaves, company-wide, month by month — with per-day verdicts (Present / Auto-leave) computed from the recorded sessions.

🔗

Feedback & HR Request

Two sidebar shortcuts open admin-configured external forms (Google Forms, ClickUp…) in an embedded popup. Links are set under Other Settings; leaving one empty disables that shortcut.

How a workday is recorded

Signing into the app never marks attendance — only the timer does. One work session exists per person per day, stored as an ordered event log.

1 · CHECK INStart your dayThe timer starts from this moment.
2 · PAUSE / RESUMEBreaks don't countPause any number of times; paused minutes are never worked time.
3 · CHECK OUTEnd your dayWorked time = the sum of all running intervals.
4 · AUTO-CLOSEForgot to check out?The day finalizes itself at midnight with whatever was recorded.
The 8-hour rule: when a day closes, ≥ 8 recorded hours marks it Present and anything less automatically marks it Auto-leave — reflected in history, dashboards and the calendar. The threshold lives in one config value, not scattered through the code.

A request can be a full day, a first/second half day (with the working-hours window snapping to that half), a date range, or hand-picked custom dates — up to 31 per request; consecutive picks are combined and separate blocks are filed as separate requests. Announcements, notifications & teams

One communication pipeline with precise targeting — nothing is broadcast wider than intended.

🎯

Four targeting modes

Company-wide (admin), per role (admin), a manager's own reporting subtree, or named teams a manager defines from their subtree. The server validates the audience on every send.

🔔

Where they land

Every recipient gets the announcement in their notifications page and the bell drawer, with an unread badge in the sidebar until it's seen.

📬

Sent & read state

Senders manage what they've sent — see it, edit their list, and track who has read each announcement.

👥

Teams

Managers group any subset of their reporting subtree into a named team, then target announcements at it. Team membership is validated against the org tree — no reaching outside your subtree.

Design decisions worth knowing

Event log as source of truth
Worked time is always recomputed from stored check-in / pause / resume / check-out events; the on-screen timer is display-only, so it can't drift or be tampered with. Refreshing, switching devices or re-logging never loses time.
Lazy finalization — no cron dependency
Stale sessions auto-close on the next read, so the 8-hour verdict never depends on a scheduled job firing. A real scheduler can still call the same finalizer (npm run finalize or the /api/cron route) for punctual midnight closes.
One org-tree field
The whole hierarchy derives from a single managerId per person. Upward and downward walks are cycle-safe and power announcements targeting, approvals scoping and team validation — there is no separate org chart to keep in sync.
Security posture
Identical error for wrong email vs wrong password; deleted users rejected even with valid tokens; password hashes never serialized; expired sessions handled in exactly one place; Helmet security headers and login rate-limiting on the API; sensitive profile fields gated per viewer.
Balance deducted only on approval
Applying for leave never touches your balance — the deduction happens when the manager approves, and the balance is re-checked at that moment. Two overlapping requests can both sit pending, but they can never overdraw the quota together.

FAQ

How do I get an account?
Registration is invite-only. The HR admin adds you from the People page, which creates your account as a pending invite, and shares your single-use invite link. Open it at /signup, set your own password (plus any personal details), and you're signed straight in. Nobody can register without an admin's invite, and invite links expire after 7 days.
Does signing in mark me present?
No. Attendance is recorded only by the timer — you must check in. When your day closes with ≥ 8 recorded hours it's marked Present; anything less becomes Auto-leave.
What happens if I forget to check out?
The session auto-closes at midnight with whatever was recorded, and the 8-hour verdict applies to that total. Stale sessions are also finalized lazily on the next read, so nothing depends on a scheduler running.
Can I take half days or scattered dates?
Yes — first half or second half of a day (single-date requests), or custom dates anywhere in the current year, up to 31 per request. Consecutive picks are merged; separate blocks become separate requests so each can be decided independently.
How far ahead can I apply for leave?
From today up to December 31 of the current year. The date picker won't offer later dates, the form validates typed dates, and the server rejects anything outside the year.
Who sees my personal details?
Sensitive profile fields (date of birth, address, phone, Aadhar) are only returned to viewers who are authorized — you, your manager chain where appropriate, and admins. Password hashes are never serialized anywhere.
Which announcement will I receive?
Anything targeted at the whole company, your role, a reporting subtree you belong to, or a team you're in. Everything arrives in your notifications feed with an unread badge until you've seen it.
