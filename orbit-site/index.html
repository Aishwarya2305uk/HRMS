Attendance tracking

A check-in timer records your day from server-side events — check in, pause for breaks, resume, check out. It survives refreshes and re-logins because time is always computed on the server.

🌿

Leave management

Sick, casual and earned leave with live balances. Full days, half days, date ranges or hand-picked custom dates — balances are deducted only on approval, never at submit time.

🏠

Work from home

A location change, not time off — WFH never touches leave balances. The approval flow is identical to leave, and a reason is required so the manager has context.

✅

Approvals

Managers get a live queue of pending leave and WFH from their direct reports, with identity, dates and balance context on every card — approve or reject with an optional comment.

📣

Announcements

Company-wide, per role, to a reporting subtree, or to custom teams — with read tracking on everything sent. The server validates the audience on every send.

🔔

Notifications

A dedicated notifications page plus a quick-glance bell drawer, with unread badges in the sidebar. Pending work and received announcements land in one feed.

👥

People directory

Search every employee with designation, department, manager and photo. Admins onboard people, set roles and reassign managers right from the roster.

🏢

Organization tree

Built purely from who reports to whom and updated the moment someone is added or reassigned. Nodes show live presence — online, idle or offline — from today's timer activity.

📅

Shared calendar

Approved leave and WFH plotted month by month, so anyone can see team availability at a glance and scheduling around absences is never a surprise.

Leave policies

Admins manage leave types, annual quotas and employment types on a dedicated page; balances update everywhere the moment a policy or approval changes.

Personal KPIs, streaks, weekly charts and a 90-day heatmap — plus company-wide attendance and leave views for admins, month by month.

Inside Orbit

Built carefully, where it counts

The engineering decisions that make Orbit trustworthy — expand any of them.

React + ViteExpressSupabase Postgres JWT authbcryptCloudflare TurnstileServerless-ready
The event log is the source of truth
Worked time is always recomputed from stored check-in / pause / resume / check-out events; the on-screen timer is display-only, so it can't drift or be tampered with. Refreshing, switching devices or re-logging never loses a minute.
No cron dependency — days finalize themselves
Stale sessions auto-close on the next read, so the 8-hour verdict never depends on a scheduled job firing on time. A real scheduler can still call the same finalizer for punctual midnight closes — belt and suspenders, not a requirement.
One field builds the whole org chart
The entire hierarchy derives from each person's immediate manager. Upward and downward walks are cycle-safe and power announcement targeting, approvals scoping and team validation — there is no separate org chart to keep in sync.
Security posture
Identical error for wrong email vs. wrong password (no user enumeration); deleted users rejected even with valid tokens; password hashes never leave the server; expired sessions handled in exactly one place; sensitive profile fields (DOB, address, phone, ID numbers) only returned to authorized viewers; every role rule re-checked server-side on every request.
Balances deducted only on approval
Applying for leave never touches your balance — the deduction happens when the manager approves, and the balance is re-checked at that moment. Two overlapping requests can both sit pending, but they can never overdraw the quota together.
Precise announcement targeting
Four audience scopes — company-wide, per role, a manager's reporting subtree, or a named team. Authoring rights are stricter than receiving rights: managers can only target their own subtree and their own teams, and the server validates the audience on every send. Nothing is broadcast wider than intended.
Fast by design
No heavy UI frameworks — hand-rolled CSS and a lean bundle. Skeleton loaders, optimistic updates, lazy per-section data loading, an offline banner, haptic feedback on supported devices and reduced-motion support throughout.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

How do I get an account?
Orbit accounts are created by your organization's HR admin — there's no public sign-up. Ask your admin (or the person who runs Orbit at your company) to add you, and you'll sign in with the email they registered.
Does signing in mark me present?
No. Attendance is recorded only by the timer — you must check in. When your day closes with ≥ 8 recorded hours it's marked Present; anything less becomes Auto-leave.
What happens if I forget to check out?
The session auto-closes at midnight with whatever was recorded, and the 8-hour verdict applies to that total. Nothing depends on you remembering.
Can I take half days or scattered dates?
Yes — first half or second half of a day, a date range, or custom dates anywhere in the current year, up to 31 per request. Consecutive picks are merged; separate blocks become separate requests so each can be decided independently.
What's the difference between leave and WFH?
Work-from-home is a location change, not time off — it never touches your leave balance. It still goes to your manager for approval, with a required reason so they have context.
Who approves my requests?
Your immediate manager sees every pending request in their approvals queue, with your identity, the dates and your remaining balance. You're notified as soon as they decide.
Who sees my personal details?
Sensitive profile fields (date of birth, address, phone, ID numbers) are only returned to authorized viewers — you and your admins. Password hashes are never sent anywhere, ever.
